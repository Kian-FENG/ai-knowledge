# Our Investment in Arda

Traditional factory automation often requires substantial engineering work whenever a new product or component changes assembly requirements. Manufacturers must update instructions, equipment configurations, workflows, and quality-control processes.

Arda is developing AI software to help automate those steps.

### Manufacturing becomes a software problem

Arda is already working with defense technology company Anduril, where its technology is deployed as part of the ArsenalOS manufacturing environment. The company is also pursuing opportunities involving electronics, defense manufacturing, and the data center supply chain.

Its longer-term ambition is to enable robotic assembly systems that can adapt when product designs change. That capability remains a development objective rather than an established commercial result. The funding is significant because modern manufacturing increasingly faces a trade-off between automation and flexibility.

Highly automated lines can perform repetitive tasks efficiently, but they may be expensive to reconfigure. Human workers can often adapt to product changes more easily, although labor availability and training create other constraints. Arda is attempting to narrow that gap.

For investors, the potential advantage combines AI software, manufacturing expertise, and accumulated experience from real factory deployments.

The company’s challenge will be demonstrating that its technology consistently reduces engineering time and production costs across different manufacturing environments.
