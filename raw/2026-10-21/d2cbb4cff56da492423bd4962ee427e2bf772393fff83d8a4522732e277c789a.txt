L9: # Title:Understanding KV Residency in Production-Scale Agentic LLM Workloads
L10: 
L11: Authors:cite5†Mayank Arya , cite6†Agrim Bari , cite7†Anjaly Parayil , cite8†Yogesh Simmhan , cite9†Renee St. Amant , cite10†Victor Rühle , cite11†Saravan Rajmohan L12: 
L13: View a PDF of the paper titled Understanding KV Residency in Production-Scale Agentic LLM Workloads, by Mayank Arya and 6 other authors
L14: 
L15: cite12†View PDF L16: > Abstract:Agentic large language model (LLM) applications turn one user request into a sequence of model and tool calls over a growing context. Retaining this context's key-value (KV) state in GPU memory lets later calls reuse it instead of recomputing it, but at fleet scale this needs substantial memory. We characterize four production Microsoft M365 workload traces (Deep Research, Enterprise Assistant, Coding Agent and Enterprise Chat) spanning more than 121 million model calls.
L17: In Deep Research, the application issues 89% of model calls and the median context grows from about 10K to 68K tokens by the 30th call. About 80% of context tokens are served from the prefix cache. With the public Llama-2-70B KV layout standing in for confidential models, one 99th-percentile Deep Research session exceeds the 80 GB HBM of an NVIDIA H100, and holding all live sessions at their peak needs an estimated 501.8 TB of HBM, although inference occupies only 5.6% of that retention time.
L18: Trace-driven replay shows that a one-hour idle cap cuts this ceiling by 3.6x while raising the share of calls that recompute context only from 4.07% to 4.53%. Under tighter budgets, eviction alone is insufficient: at one tenth of the busiest hour's demand, LRU eviction makes 63% of Deep Research calls recompute and clairvoyant furthest-next-use 31%, whereas multi-resolution retention (MRR), which demotes the LRU session's KV state to int4, lowers this to 13.6%.
L19: Placement imposes a separate limit: when the next call reaches a different deployment, cached-context reuse drops from 89% to 29%, and treating KV state as deployment-local raises the recompute floor under unlimited memory from 5.4% to 20.2%. These findings motivate retention that changes the representation of KV state, together with deployment-aware routing or migration. We plan to release AgenticTraceSim, a parametric trace generator, and a residency replay framework.

L9: # Title:Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization
L10: 
L11: Authors:cite5†Zhepei Wei , cite6†Wenlin Yao , cite7†Baolin Peng , cite8†Danqing Wang , cite9†Qianhui Wu , cite10†Hao Cheng , cite11†Yu Meng , cite12†Jianfeng Gao L12: 
L13: View a PDF of the paper titled Self-Evolving Coding Agents via Harness-Policy Optimization, by Zhepei Wei and 7 other authors
L14: 
L15: cite13†View PDF cite14†HTML (experimental) L16: > Abstract:Large language model (LLM) agents have shown promising potential in coding tasks, and their performance is jointly determined by the policy model and the harness that mediates their interaction with the environment. Indeed, harness and policy are mutually reinforcing and evolving only one component is suboptimal---a harness tuned for one checkpoint may not target the failure modes of an updated policy, while policy learning remains limited to the trajectories elicited by a fixed harness.
L17: In light of this, we propose Self-HPO, a recursive self-improving framework for coding agents that alternates harness and policy optimization, using the evolving policy as its own harness proposer. Specifically, in each round, the current policy first analyzes its own failures and proposes new harness candidates (H-step).
L18: The policy is then updated through reinforcement learning (RL) under the selected harness (P-step), and hard tasks identified during training form the next harness search set, keeping harness optimization targeted to the policy's evolving failure modes. Across three coding benchmarks, Self-HPO consistently outperforms harness-only and policy-only baselines, improving Qwen3.5-35B-A3B's average resolve rate from 57.8% to 64.8%, with gains generalizing to out-of-distribution settings.
L19: Further analysis shows that both optimized components retain gains even when paired with their base counterparts, while their joint gain exceeds the sum of their individual gains. Self-HPO also improves reliability (Pass@1) and expands the capability frontier (Pass@k) over the base agent. Additionally, we show that with a stronger external model (e.g., Opus 4.8) as the harness proposer, the same framework yields a further performance boost.
