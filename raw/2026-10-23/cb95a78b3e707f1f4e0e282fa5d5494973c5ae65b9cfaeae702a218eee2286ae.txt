L13: # Peppermint Raises $4.7M to Bring Scientific Precision to the Finances of Clinical Research
L14: 
L15: Company’s AI-powered platform was purpose-built for clinical trial teams and hospital systems to transform clinical research into a scalable engine for financial growth and scientific discovery
L16: SAN FRANCISCO — October 8, 2026 — 【6†Peppermint†getpeppermint.ai】, an AI-powered platform that streamlines financial operations and accelerates growth for clinical research teams, today announced $4.7 million in seed funding led by 【7†Moxxie†www.moxxie.vc】, with participation from 【8†Homebrew†www.homebrew.co】, 【9†Better Tomorrow Ventures†btv.vc】, 【10†Layout Ventures†www.layoutventures.com】, 【11†Digital Health Venture Partners†www.dhvp.io】, 【12†Night Capital,†nightcapvc.com】 and angel investors Benjamin Mann and Evan Moore.
L17: The funding will further enable the founding team, who previously built payments and reconciliation infrastructure for Robinhood, to transform clinical research into a scalable engine for financial growth and scientific discovery.
L18: Despite advances in the science and technology driving novel drug development, the industry still relies on an antiquated and fragmented financial framework. Instead of payments flowing smoothly between sponsors, CROs, health systems, and research sites, clinical research relies on a patchwork of systems that were never designed to work together.
L19: Every party absorbs the cost of that complexity. Health systems carry it across an entire research portfolio, where the finances of clinical research remain one of the least visible lines on the balance sheet. Sites feel it most acutely: One-third of sites have more than 90 days’ worth of overdue receivables, and 50% of sites have three months or less of operating capital, according to a 【13†2024 survey†myscrs.org】 by the Society for Clinical Research Sites (SCRS).
L20: Peppermint is the first AI-powered financial operations platform designed exclusively for clinical research, bringing the uncompromising precision and auditable accuracy of clinical science to its own revenue cycle.
L21: Co-founders David Freeman and VJ Thurimella met at X1, where they were the respective first business hire and first engineer. Together, they helped build the financial infrastructure behind the consumer credit card business that Robinhood acquired to launch its Gold Card.
L22: When they saw the inefficiency of financial systems behind clinical research – and how it was depriving research teams of not only cash, but visibility into how much a study actually generates in revenue, how much it costs to run, and the metrics behind both – they knew their fintech experience could break open the industry’s financial black box.
L23: “At X1 and Robinhood we helped build systems designed for precision and scale, to process billions of dollars every year,” said Freeman. “We’re bringing that architecture to clinical research, converting earnings into friction-free collections that also provide CROs and sponsors with cleaner data from the source.”

L13: # Introducing Microsoft-Decision-1, our model for fast decision-making
L14: 
L15: Our new decision-scoring model delivers top performance in latency and quality on structured decision tasks to outperform both LLMs and other decision models.
L16: 
L17: By 【3†Achint Srivastava】VP of Software Engineering, Office of the CTO, Microsoft
L18: 
L19: 2026.10.09
L20: Decision models are quickly emerging as an important new category in AI. Unlike LLMs, which are designed to generate text or reason through complex problems, decision models are purpose-built to deliver structured outputs that software can immediately act on. And once you understand that capability—making decisions and classifying things at very low cost with high performance—all kinds of useful tasks get unlocked.
L21: Today we’re introducing Microsoft-Decision-1, our new model for fast decision-scoring, available in 【4†Microsoft Foundry†ai.azure.com】 and through 【5†OpenRouter†openrouter.ai】. This model is designed for routing, classification, prioritization, verification, and workflow control, making it easier to incorporate decision intelligence into existing applications, agents, and workflows in a secure, trusted environment.
L22: Microsoft-Decision-1 delivers top performance in latency and quality on structured decision tasks to outperform both LLMs and other decision models.
L23: Microsoft-Decision-1 achieved the highest accuracy in our 36-benchmark comparison, spanning nearly 150,000 questions across benchmarks kept blind from training. And in our benchmarking, it was the fastest measured: 2.5 times quicker than H2O-Lightning-4B v1.1, the runner-up, and 35 times quicker than GPT-6 Sol.
L24: 
L25: Editor’s note: This post was updated from the original to add benchmarks for Jev on accuracy and calibration.
L26: ## How Microsoft-Decision-1 works
L27: 
L28: To build Microsoft-Decision-1, we post trained Qwen3.5-9B for fast, single-pass decision scoring and will soon rebase it on other models, including Microsoft AI (MAI) and OpenAI. When given a fixed set of answer options, Microsoft-Decision-1 provides a calibrated probability score for each option. The model supports yes/no, multiple-choice, and rating options, as well as rubric-based grading of AI responses and agent actions, all through a simple structured API call.
L29: To build a reliable decision model, we had to address several challenges:
L30: ### 1. Speed
L31: 
L32: Each decision adds delay, especially when one step depends on another. For example, adding just 100 milliseconds to each of 20 sequential decisions adds two seconds to the overall workflow.
L33: 
L34: Microsoft-Decision-1 P50 latency is ~35x faster than GPT-6 Sol.
L35: ### 2. Quality that generalizes
L36: It’s easy to overfit a model for one benchmark or one type of decision task. We need to know whether that quality carries over to various tasks the model wasn’t trained on. That’s why we evaluated Microsoft-Decision-1 across dozens of benchmarks kept blinded from training, spanning routing, ranking, long context, multilingual and out-of-distribution tasks, reasoning, and safety.
