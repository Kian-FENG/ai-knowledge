L115: # Google DeepMind and Singapore Government collaborate to build fine-tuned AI model for scam detection
L116: 
L117: JJ Geewax and Miguel de Andrés
L118: 
L119: cite42†Image: Abstract 3D design featuring wavy layers of glossy red and textured white akin to the Singapore flag, adorned with glass spheres and a pixelated red block formation†lh3.googleusercontent.com L120: 
L121: Collaborating with the Government Technology Agency of Singapore (GovTech Singapore) to protect users without compromising privacy.
L122: Scams, such as investment fraud, fake job offers, and impersonation of public officials make up over half of Singapore’s reported crimes, and account for more than cite43†S$4 billion in losses since 2019†www.straitstimes.com . At the same time, scam tactics have shifted from simple phishing to complex social engineering, whereby attackers manipulate victims with convincing conversations before requesting money. Such scams now account for up to 80% of all losses.
L123: To address this, Google DeepMind and the Government Technology Agency of Singapore (GovTech Singapore) have been working together as part of our cite44†National AI Partnership with the Singapore Government. Together, we've fine-tuned a privacy-preserving, on-device scam detection model.
L124: ## On-device AI protection without compromising privacy
L125: Generic models often struggle with local deception techniques, making scam detection harder. To address this, Google’s lightweight Gemma model was fine-tuned on local scam conversations. These ranged from impersonations of public institutions, banks, and local figures, to trending regional schemes. This ensures the fine-tuned model will be able to distinguish benign social chatter from genuine manipulation when deployed in applications installed by citizens.
L126: Unlike traditional threat detection, which relies on server-side scanning of messages in the cloud, this model can process conversation data directly on-device. When integrated into applications, this will allow faster analysis of conversation patterns to detect manipulative intent and help citizens act before financial or credential exposure can occur.
L127: The roll out of the model within applications will be carefully phased and developed in partnership with the Singapore Government. When deployed, the model will only be able to access on-device conversations with the user's consent, in accordance with the terms of use of that application. As the model runs entirely on-device, neither the Singapore Government nor Google, will be able to access users’ conversations through its deployment.
L128: > Fighting scams shouldn't mean sacrificing personal privacy. Working with Google DeepMind, we're bringing advanced AI protection directly onto citizens' devices. This allows us to catch highly targeted, local scam attempts in real time while keeping personal communications private.
L129: 
L130: Mark Chen
L131: 
L132: Director, Trust and Safety, GovTech Singapore
L133: ## Under the hood: local context and edge efficiency
L134: 
L135: When the model detects manipulative conversational patterns, it triggers a real-time warning directly on the user's device advising them to pause and exercise caution before sharing sensitive information or funds.
L136: 
L137: Running a complex scam classifier model on modern smartphones is no easy task, with battery efficiency being a critical area of concern. To do this, we're relying on a two-stage pipeline:
L138: cite45†Image: A flowchart illustrating a two-stage message screening system. Stage 1 uses an on-device "Lightweight Fast Screening Classifier" to filter incoming messages; safe messages receive a "Benign Verdict", while suspicious ones proceed to Stage 2. Stage 2 uses a "Fine-tuned Gemma Model" for multi-turn conversation analysis, resulting in either a "Scam Alert" or a "Benign Verdict."†storage.googleapis.com cite46†Image: A flowchart illustrating a two-stage message screening system.
L139: Stage 1 uses an on-device "Lightweight Fast Screening Classifier" to filter incoming messages; safe messages receive a "Benign Verdict", while suspicious ones proceed to Stage 2. Stage 2 uses a "Fine-tuned Gemma Model" for multi-turn conversation analysis, resulting in either a "Scam Alert" or a "Benign Verdict."†storage.googleapis.com L140:   * Stage 1: A lightweight, efficient classifier screens inbound messages, using minimal power, focused on catching potential threats.
L141:   * Stage 2: In-context analysis with our fine-tuned Gemma model, where we analyze a larger snapshot of the conversation to confirm a threat or discard a false alarm.
L142: ## Built on strict governance
L143: 
L144: Presented at Singapore International Cyber Week (SICW 2026), this collaboration provides a model for messaging platforms balancing user protection with end-to-end encryption. Strict governance was maintained throughout development:
L145:   * Sovereign Sandbox: Fine-tuning took place within GovTech Singapore's managed and maintained GCP environment, keeping training data isolated.
L146:   * No Citizen Data: Training strictly excluded private or personally identifiable information, utilising anonymised datasets derived from scam reports and intelligence feeds.
L147:   * Stress-Testing: The model was tested against synthetic dialogues and local corpora to cover diverse scam categories and evolving impersonation tactics.
L148: ## The future of on-device safety
L149: The project demonstrates how AI can help counter criminality without compromising safety or privacy. It also shows how governments and model providers can work together to build tech for public good. With this lightweight, on-device model, we've proven the ability for local, real-time scam detection while ensuring chat interactions remain secure.
L150: We look forward to sharing more soon on our collaboration with the Singapore government, as well as exploring the potential for similar technology to be deployed in other countries.
