L22: ## From demo to production.
L23: 
L24: AI has made it possible to build capable agents faster than ever, but most never make it into production. The challenge is no longer getting an agent to work once. Rather, it is getting that agent to perform real work continuously, take actions, and scale without becoming unreliable, uncontrollable, or economically unsustainable.

L27: ## Infrastructure at the point of execution.
L28: 
L29: Sapiom removes the infrastructure barriers between a working agent demo and an agent running in production. The first barrier it is addressing is cost. Most agents run on infrastructure choices made once during development and rarely revisited. They execute through the same static stack for every task, even as requirements, prices, performance, and availability change. Instead of choosing the best path at runtime, they remain locked into yesterday’s decisions.
L30: Sapiom sits at the point of execution, turning fixed infrastructure choices into real-time decisions. For every action, it selects the best allowed path across models, compute, tools, and services based on the requirements of the task, cost, quality, latency, reliability, and company policy. Budgets and permissions are enforced before execution, and every action is metered and recorded in a complete audit trail.
L31: ## Sapiom Router, Agent Studio, and Sapiom Runtime.
L32: 
L33: Alongside the financing, Sapiom is launching Sapiom Router, Agent Studio, and Sapiom Runtime.
L34:   * Router matches each model call to the right model instead of defaulting to the most expensive one.
L35:   * Agent Studio gives engineering teams a local environment to build, test, inspect, and deploy agents with the context of their existing codebase.
L36:   * Runtime provides the managed production infrastructure where those agents execute with the access, routing, recovery, step-level visibility, and controls required to operate at scale.
L37: ## Operating at scale.
